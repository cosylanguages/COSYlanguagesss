<section class="verb-section" id="v-be"> <div class="verb-head"> <div class="verb-num">01</div> <div> <h2>🧍 To Be</h2> <p class="verb-tagline">The very first verb every beginner needs — and the one where languages disagree the most.</p> </div> </div> <div class="grammar-note"> <p><strong>The trap:</strong> English uses <em>to be</em> for age, hunger and thirst (“I <b>am</b> 25”, “I <b>am</b> hungry”). Most other COSY languages don't. Watch what each language really does:</p><ul><li><strong>Age uses HAVE, not BE</strong>, in French, Italian, Spanish and Portuguese — « j'<b>ai</b> 25 ans », « <b>ho</b> 25 anni », « <b>tengo</b> 25 años », « <b>tenho</b> 25 anos » (literally “I have 25 years”).</li><li><strong>Age uses BE</strong> in German and Greek, just like English — „Ich <b>bin</b> 25 Jahre alt“, « Είμαι 25 χρονών ».</li><li><strong>Age uses neither</strong> in Russian: <em>«Мне 25 лет»</em> literally means “to-me 25 years” — there is no verb at all in the present tense sentence.</li><li><strong>Hunger/thirst use HAVE</strong> in French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese <em>and German</em> — « j'<b>ai</b> faim », « <b>ho</b> fame », « <b>tengo</b> hambre », « <b>tenho</b> fome », „Ich <b>habe</b> Hunger“.</li><li><strong>Greek has no BE or HAVE for hunger/thirst</strong> — it uses two single dedicated verbs: <em>πεινάω</em> (to-be-hungry) and <em>διψάω</em> (to-be-thirsty).</li><li><strong>Russian usually drops “to be” entirely</strong> in the present tense — «Я Анна» (“I Anna”) is a complete, correct sentence.</li></ul><p>Always double-check the <em>age / hunger / thirst</em> pattern per language before teaching it as a direct translation of English — it is the single biggest false-friend trap at A0.</p> </div> <h3 class="sub-h">The verb in all 14 COSY languages</h3> <p class="grid-help">Each word links straight to its source entry in the <a href="https://github.com/cosylanguages/COSYdata" target="_blank" rel="noopener">COSYdata</a> repository.
