Top 10 Overused Verbs and Their Precise Synonyms for the Intermediate Plateau

Break through the B1–B2 intermediate plateau by replacing overused safe verbs like get, make, do, and think with precise, high-impact synonyms.

Read in: 🇬🇷 EL 🇫🇷 FR 🇮🇹 IT 🇷🇺 RU 🇬🇧 EN
J Written by JY DM
📅 2026-09-11 ⏱️ 7 min read B1–B2 EPISODE 4

One of the most clear indicators that a learner has reached the **intermediate plateau (roughly B1–B2)** is a heavy reliance on a small cluster of "safe" verbs for almost everything.

These fundamental verbs aren't "bad"—they are essential structural blocks of the English language. However, when you use them repeatedly across every sentence, speech can sound repetitive and vague. Expanding these core verbs into precise alternatives immediately elevates your fluency, making your communication clearer, more vivid, and far more natural.

The Top 10 Overused Verbs (And Their Precise Alternatives)

When learners hit B1–B2, these ten verbs tend to carry 80% of their spoken output. Here is how you can expand each one:

1. Get

**receive**: *I received your email yesterday.*
**obtain / acquire**: *The team obtained the necessary license.*
**become**: *It gets cold → It becomes chilly.*
**reach / arrive**: *We will reach our destination by noon.*
**fetch / collect**: *Could you fetch the documents?*
**earn / gain**: *She earned a reputation for reliability.*

2. Make

  • **create / produce**: *The factory produces renewable components.*
  • **build / construct**: *They constructed a new facility.*
  • **develop / establish**: *We established a new working agreement.*
  • **generate**: *The initiative generated significant interest.*
  • **design / compose**: *She composed a thoughtful report.*

3. Do

  • **perform / carry out**: *The team carried out an extensive audit.*
  • **complete / accomplish**: *He accomplished his quarterly targets.*
  • **execute / conduct**: *We conducted a series of market tests.*
  • **handle / manage**: *She handled the situation calmly.*
  • **undertake / practice**: *They undertook a new research initiative.*

4. Have

  • **own / possess**: *The company possesses valuable intellectual property.*
  • **contain / hold**: *The report contains critical findings.*
  • **experience / enjoy**: *We experienced great progress this semester.*
  • **include**: *The package includes all essential amenities.*
  • **maintain / keep**: *He maintains a structured routine.*

5. Go

  • **travel / move**: *They traveled across Europe last summer.*
  • **head / proceed**: *Please proceed to Gate 4.*
  • **leave / depart**: *The train departs at 08:30.*
  • **visit / attend**: *She attended the annual conference.*

6. Say

  • **tell / mention**: *He mentioned the upcoming deadline.*
  • **explain / state**: *The official stated the facts clearly.*
  • **claim / argue**: *Researchers argue that early intervention works.*
  • **admit / announce**: *The directors announced a new policy.*
  • **suggest / point out**: *She pointed out an important error.*

7. Think

  • **believe / suppose**: *I believe this is the best course of action.*
  • **assume / suspect**: *We assume the shipment will arrive on time.*
  • **consider / reflect**: *Let us consider all available options.*
  • **imagine / wonder**: *I wonder what the final outcome will be.*
  • **realize / conclude**: *We concluded that further study was required.*

8. Look

  • **watch / observe**: *Scientists observed the specimen closely.*
  • **notice / examine**: *The inspector examined the apparatus.*
  • **stare / gaze / glance**: *She glanced at her watch during the meeting.*
  • **inspect / appear / seem**: *The preliminary results appear promising.*

9. Know

  • **understand / recognize**: *She recognized the significance of the event.*
  • **realize / remember**: *I realized the answer during the presentation.*
  • **discover / identify**: *They identified the root cause of the delay.*
  • **be aware of / be familiar with**: *Are you familiar with this protocol?*
  • **master**: *He mastered the technique after months of practice.*

10. Give

  • **provide / offer**: *We offer comprehensive guidance.*
  • **hand / deliver**: *She delivered the speech with confidence.*
  • **donate / award / grant**: *The board granted approval for the budget.*
  • **present / supply / lend**: *The supplier provides high-quality goods.*

Verbs That Make Intermediate Learners Sound Repetitive

Beyond the big ten, teachers routinely spot three additional verbs overused in almost every lesson:

**Like** → *enjoy, appreciate, prefer, admire, value, be fond of, be keen on, be passionate about* - *Instead of:* "I like reading history books." - *Try:* "I am passionate about studying history."
**Use** → *apply, employ, utilize, operate, handle, implement, adopt, rely on* - *Instead of:* "We use this software." - *Try:* "We implemented this software across our team."
**Put** → *place, set, lay, install, arrange, position, store, insert, attach* - *Instead of:* "Put the files on the desk." - *Try:* "Arrange the documents on the table."

Academic & Professional Upgrades: "Instead of... Try..."

In professional and academic environments, replacing informal verbs with precise action verbs instantly boosts credibility:

Instead of...
Try using...
Example Sentence
Instead of **get**
Try *obtain, acquire, receive*
"We **obtained** approval from the board."
Instead of **show**
Try *demonstrate, illustrate, reveal*
"The data **demonstrates** a steady growth."
Instead of **help**
Try *assist, support, facilitate*
"This tool **facilitates** remote collaboration."
Instead of **fix**
Try *repair, resolve, solve*
"Our team **resolved** the technical issue."
Instead of **change**
Try *alter, modify, transform, adjust*
"We **modified** the schedule slightly."
Instead of **start**
Try *begin, launch, initiate, introduce*
"The company **launched** a new product."
Instead of **stop**
Try *cease, end, halt, discontinue*
"Operations were **halted** temporarily."
Instead of **find**
Try *discover, identify, locate, detect*
"Engineers **identified** the defect."
Instead of **tell**
Try *inform, notify, explain, describe*
"We **notified** clients regarding the change."
Instead of **ask**
Try *request, inquire, question, consult*
"She **inquired** about the job opening."

Quick Level-Up Rules for Daily Conversation & Social Media

Want quick, bite-sized upgrades you can put into practice immediately? Use these "Stop Saying" upgrades:

  1. **Stop saying GET money all the time:** - ✅ *earn money* - ✅ *receive funding* - ✅ *collect revenue* - ✅ *obtain grants*
  2. **Stop saying MAKE a company:** - ✅ *establish a company* - ✅ *found a enterprise* - ✅ *launch a business* - ✅ *build an organization*
  3. **Stop saying GO to another country:** - ✅ *travel abroad* - ✅ *relocate overseas* - ✅ *move internationally* - ✅ *visit another nation*
  4. **Stop saying I THINK all the time:** - ✅ *I believe...* - ✅ *I would argue...* - ✅ *I assume...* - ✅ *In my estimation...*

🌐 Multilingual Adaptations for FR, RU, IT & EL Learners

The intermediate plateau isn't unique to English. If you are studying French, Russian, Italian, or Greek, you will recognize these exact same overused "safe" verbs and their B1–B2 upgrades:

🇫🇷 French (Avoir, Faire, Dire, Penser, Donner)

**Overused *Avoir*:** *avoir un problème* → *rencontrer une difficulté* / *faire face à un problème*
**Overused *Faire*:** *faire une décision* ❌ → *prendre une décision* ✅ • *faire du progrès* → *accomplir des progrès*
**Overused *Dire*:** *dire une idée* → *exprimer une idée* / *suggérer une solution*
**Overused *Penser*:** *je pense que...* → *je considère que...* / *à mon sens...* / *j'estime que...*

🇷🇺 Russian (Делать, Сказать, Думать, Получить, Иметь)

**Overused *Делать*:** *делать решение* ❌ → *принимать/принять решение* ✅ • *делать работу* → *выполнять/выполнить задачу*
**Overused *Сказать*:** *он сказал мне* → *он сообщил мне* / *отметил* / *утверждает*
**Overused *Думать*:** *я думаю* → *я полагаю* / *считаю* / *на мой взгляд*
**Overused *Получить*:** *получить опыт* → *приобрести опыт* / *накопить опыт*

🇮🇹 Italian (Fare, Dire, Pensare, Avere, Mettere)

**Overused *Fare*:** *fare una scelta* → *compiere una scelta* • *fare una domanda* → *porre una domanda*
**Overused *Dire*:** *dire un'opinione* → *esprimere un parere* / *sostenere che...*
**Overused *Pensare*:** *penso che...* → *ritengo che...* / *ritengo sia opportuno...* / *a mio avviso...*
**Overused *Avere*:** *avere un dubbio* → *nutrire un dubbio* / *nutrire perplessità*

🇬🇷 Greek (Кάνω, Λέω, Σκέφτομαι, Έχω, Παίρνω)

**Overused *Κάνω*:** *κάνω μια απόφαση* ❌ → *παίρνω μια απόφαση* ✅ • *κάνω πρόοδο* → *σημειώνω πρόοδο*
**Overused *Λέω*:** *λέω μια γνώμη* → *εκφράζω μια γνώμη* / *υποστηρίζω ότι...*
**Overused *Σκέφτομαι*:** *σκέφτομαι ότι...* → *θεωρώ ότι...* / *πιστεύω πως...* / *εκτιμώ ότι...*

Final Thought: Evolution, Not Elimination

Replacing overused verbs is not about banishing words like *get* or *make* completely. They remain indispensable in native speech. The goal is recognizing when a specific verb conveys your meaning more accurately, helping you transition smoothly from B1 intermediate conversation to B2–C1 advanced precision.
